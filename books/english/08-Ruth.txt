(Ruth 1:1) 
During the time when the judges ruled the land, there was a great famine in the land of Israel. So, a man from Bethlehem in Judah left his home to live as a foreigner in the country of Moab, along with his wife and his two sons.

(Ruth 1:2) 
The man’s name was Elimelech, and his wife’s name was Naomi. They had two sons, one named Mahlon and the other Chilion. They were Ephrathites, from Bethlehem in Judah.

(Ruth 1:3) 
Naomi’s husband, Elimelech, died there in Moab. So, she was left with her two sons.

(Ruth 1:4) 
Her sons took wives from among the Moabite women. One wife was named Orpah, and the other was named Ruth. They lived in Moab for about ten years.

(Ruth 1:5) 
Then both Mahlon and Chilion also died. So, Naomi was left without her two sons and her husband, completely alone.

(Ruth 1:6) 
Naomi then decided to return from the country of Moab to her homeland, along with her two daughters-in-law. She had heard that the Lord God had visited His people in Judah and had given them food.

(Ruth 1:7) 
So, she and her two daughters-in-law set out from the place where they had been living as foreigners. They were already on their way back to the land of Judah.

(Ruth 1:8) 
Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, 'Go, return each of you to your mother’s house. May the Lord God deal kindly with you, just as you have dealt kindly with your dead husbands and with me.'

(Ruth 1:9) 
'May the Lord God grant each of you rest and peace in the home of a future husband whom you will marry.' Then she kissed them goodbye, and they began to weep loudly.

(Ruth 1:10) 
They said to her, 'No, we will go with you to your people.'

(Ruth 1:11) 
But Naomi answered them, 'Turn back, my daughters. Why would you come with me? Do I still have sons in my womb who could become your husbands?'

(Ruth 1:12) 
'Go back, my daughters, go your way. For I am too old to have a husband. Even if I were to say, “I have hope,” even if I were to become pregnant tonight and give birth to sons—'

(Ruth 1:13) 
'would you wait until they grew up? Would you refrain from marrying for them? No, my daughters, please don’t. For my distress is far greater than yours, because the Lord has turned against me.'

(Ruth 1:14) 
Again, they wept loudly. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye and returned to her own people. But Ruth clung to Naomi.

(Ruth 1:15) 
Then Naomi said to Ruth, 'Look, your sister-in-law has gone back to her own people and to her gods. You should go back with her.'

(Ruth 1:16) 
But Ruth replied, 'Do not urge me to leave you or to turn back from following you. For wherever you go, I will go, and wherever you live, I will live. Your people shall be my people, and your God shall be my God.'

(Ruth 1:17) 
'Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the Lord God do to me and more also, if anything but death separates you and me.'

(Ruth 1:18) 
When Naomi saw that Ruth was determined to go with her, she stopped arguing with her or trying to persuade her otherwise.

(Ruth 1:19) 
So, the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. When they arrived in the city, the entire city was stirred because of them, and the women exclaimed, 'Can this be Naomi?'

(Ruth 1:20) 
She replied to them, 'Do not call me Naomi, which means 'pleasant.' Instead, call me Mara, which means 'bitter.' For the Almighty God has filled me with much bitterness and sorrow.'

(Ruth 1:21) 
'I went out from here full, but the Lord God has brought me back empty. Why do you call me Naomi, when the Lord God has humbled me, and the Almighty God has brought calamity upon me?'

(Ruth 1:22) 
So Naomi returned with her daughter-in-law Ruth, the Moabitess, from the country of Moab. They arrived in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

(Ruth 2:1) 
Now Naomi had a relative of her husband, a man of great influence and noble lineage, from the family of Elimelech, named Boaz. This man possessed significant wealth and social standing.

(Ruth 2:2) 
And Ruth, the Moabite woman, said to her mother-in-law, "If you permit it, I will go into a field and gather the fallen grain that the reapers leave behind, wherever I may find favor with a landowner." To this Naomi responded, "Go, my daughter." This act of gleaning was a provision for the poor.

(Ruth 2:3) 
So she went out, and coming to a field, she gathered the grain behind the reapers. It just so happened that the field belonged to the man named Boaz, who was from the family of Elimelech. This was truly providential.

(Ruth 2:4) 
And behold, Boaz himself was coming from Bethlehem. He said to the reapers, "The Lord be with you." And they responded to him, "May the Lord bless you." This exchange showed the respectful and pious nature of their community.

(Ruth 2:5) 
Then Boaz said to the young man who was in charge of the reapers, "Whose young woman is this?" He noticed her as a stranger in his field.

(Ruth 2:6) 
To whom the young man responded, "This is the young Moabite woman who came with Naomi from the region of Moab." Her foreign origin was known.

(Ruth 2:7) 
"And she asked to gather the grain behind the reapers' footsteps, and to glean among the gathered sheaves. And since we came here, until now, she has diligently persevered in the field, and she has not even returned home for a moment." This demonstrated Ruth's exceptional work ethic.

(Ruth 2:8) 
Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Listen, my daughter, do not go into another field to gather grain, nor depart from this place. Instead, stay close to my young women." This was a direct offer of protection and special privilege.

(Ruth 2:9) 
"And wherever my reapers have finished, follow after them. For I have commanded my young men not to bother you. Rather, when you are thirsty, go to the water vessels and drink from the water from which my young men also drink." Boaz extended hospitality beyond mere permission.

(Ruth 2:10) 
She, falling on her face and prostrating herself on the ground, said, "How have I deserved this, that I should find such favor in your eyes, I who am a mere foreigner?" Ruth's humility was evident.

(Ruth 2:11) 
To whom Boaz responded, "All things have been reported to me: what you have done for your mother-in-law after the death of your husband, and how you left your parents and your native land, and came to a people whom you did not know before." Boaz was aware of her virtuous actions.

(Ruth 2:12) 
"May the Lord repay you for your work, and may you receive a full reward from the Lord God of Israel, to whom you have come and under whose protective wings you have taken refuge." Boaz's blessing recognized her conversion to the God of Israel.

(Ruth 2:13) 
And she said, "I have found favor in your eyes, my lord, for you have comforted me and spoken kindly to the heart of your handmaid, though I am not even like one of your own servants." She felt unworthy of such generosity.

(Ruth 2:14) 
And Boaz said to her, "When it is time for the midday meal, come here, and eat some bread, and dip your morsel in the vinegar." So she sat beside the reapers, and he offered her bread, and she ate and was satisfied, and took some of the leftovers to save for later.

(Ruth 2:15) 
And she rose up to continue gleaning. Boaz then commanded his young men, saying, "Even if she wishes to glean among the sheaves already gathered, do not hinder her." This was an extraordinary allowance for a gleaner.

(Ruth 2:16) 
"But rather, from your own gathered sheaves, intentionally throw out some grain and leave it for her, so that she may collect it without any shame, and no one should rebuke her." This made her task easier and more dignified.

(Ruth 2:17) 
So she gleaned in the field until evening. And she beat out the grain she had gathered to separate it from the stalks, and she found it amounted to about an ephah of barley. This was a substantial amount, thanks to Boaz's generosity.

(Ruth 2:18) 
And carrying what she had collected, she returned to the city and showed it to her mother-in-law. Moreover, she brought forth and gave Naomi some of the leftover food with which she herself had been satisfied earlier. Ruth's constant care for Naomi was clear.

(Ruth 2:19) 
And her mother-in-law said to her, "Where did you glean today, and where did you do your work? Blessed be he who had mercy on you." She told Naomi where she had worked, and she said the name of the man under whom she had worked was Boaz.

(Ruth 2:20) 
To whom Naomi responded, "May he be blessed by the Lord, because he has shown the same kindness, as before, now also to the living and to the dead." Naomi was referring to the potential preservation of their family line. And she added, "The man is our close relative, and from our family." This revealed Boaz's role as a potential kinsman-redeemer.

(Ruth 2:21) 
And Ruth said, "Furthermore, he also commanded me this: that I should stay close to his reapers until all the harvest is completely finished." Boaz's protection and provision were ongoing.

(Ruth 2:22) 
To whom her mother-in-law said, "It is better, my daughter, that you go out to glean with his young women, lest you suffer any trouble or harassment in another field." Naomi wisely advised Ruth to remain under Boaz's protection.

(Ruth 2:23) 
So she remained with the young women of Boaz and gleaned until both the barley harvest and the wheat harvest were finished. Ruth obediently continued her work, patiently awaiting God's plan.

(Ruth 3:1) 
Then Naomi, her mother-in-law, said to her, "My daughter, shouldn't I find you a safe place to settle down, and provide for you?"

(Ruth 3:2) 
"Look, Boaz, with whom you worked in the field, he is a relative of ours, and tonight he is winnowing barley at the threshing floor."

(Ruth 3:3) 
"So, wash yourself, anoint yourself with oil, and put on your best clothes, and go down to the threshing floor. Do not let any man see you until he has finished eating and drinking."

(Ruth 3:4) 
"When he lies down to sleep, observe the place where he sleeps. Then go and uncover the covering at his feet, and lie down there. And he will then take action regarding your situation, and he will then depart."

(Ruth 3:5) 
Ruth replied, "Whatever you command me, I will do."

(Ruth 3:6) 
So she went down to the threshing floor and did everything her mother-in-law had commanded her.

(Ruth 3:7) 
When Boaz had eaten and drunk and was in good spirits, and had gone to lie down at the edge of the threshing floor, Ruth came secretly. She uncovered the covering at his feet and lay down there to sleep.

(Ruth 3:8) 
And it happened in the middle of the night: the man was startled and became agitated, and he saw a woman lying at his feet.

(Ruth 3:9) 
And he said to her, "Who are you?" She answered, "I am Ruth, your handmaid. Spread the corner of your cloak over your handmaid, for you are a near kinsman and have the right to redeem me."

(Ruth 3:10) 
He said, "Blessed are you by the Lord, my daughter! This latter kindness you have shown is even greater than the former, because you have not pursued young men, whether poor or rich."

(Ruth 3:11) 
"Do not fear, therefore; whatever you ask of me, I will do for you. For all the people who dwell in the gate of my city know that you are a virtuous woman."

(Ruth 3:12) 
"And I do not wish to hinder this plan, for there is a kinsman nearer than I who has the first right of redemption."

(Ruth 3:13) 
"Stay here tonight, and when morning comes, if he wishes to take you as his wife, it is well. But if he does not wish to take you, I will certainly take you, as the Lord lives! Sleep here until morning."

(Ruth 3:14) 
So she slept at his feet until near daybreak, and rose before people could recognize one another. And Boaz said, "Be careful that no one knows you came here."

(Ruth 3:15) 
And again he said, "Spread out your shawl with which you are covered, and hold it with both hands." When she had spread it out and held it, he measured out six measures of barley and placed it upon her. And she then went into the city.

(Ruth 3:16) 
And she came to her mother-in-law, who said to her, "How did you fare, my daughter?" And she told her everything the man had done for her.

(Ruth 3:17) 
And she said, "He gave me these six measures of barley, and he said, 'Do not return empty-handed to your mother-in-law.'"

(Ruth 3:18) 
Then Naomi said, "Wait, my daughter, until we see what outcome this matter will have. For the man will not rest until he has completed his word and settled the business today."

(Ruth 4:1) 
So Boaz went up to the city gate, which was where all legal and social matters were decided, and he sat there. When he saw the close relative—the one he had spoken about concerning the land and Ruth—passing by, he called out to him by name and said, "Step aside for a moment, and sit down here with me." So the kinsman turned aside from the path and sat down with Boaz.

(Ruth 4:2) 
Boaz then gathered ten men from among the elders of the city—these were the necessary witnesses for legal transactions—and said to them, "Please sit down here."

(Ruth 4:3) 
Once they were all seated, Boaz spoke to the close relative, explaining the situation: "Naomi, who has returned from the land of Moab, is selling the portion of the field that belonged to our relative Elimelech."

(Ruth 4:4) 
"I thought it was necessary to tell you this now, and to inform you in the presence of all these sitting here, especially the elders of the city. If you wish to redeem the property by your right as a close relative, then buy it and take possession of it. But if you do not wish to redeem it, then you must let me know so that I can proceed, because I am the next in line to you to exercise the right of a close relative to redeem the land. Therefore, there is no other person besides you who has the right of redemption, and I am next after you." The kinsman immediately responded, "I will redeem it."

(Ruth 4:5) 
Boaz then explained, "When you buy the field from Naomi, you must also take Ruth the Moabitess as your wife, she who was the wife of the deceased. This is to ensure that the name of the deceased man is continued in his inheritance and does not perish."

(Ruth 4:6) 
The kinsman replied, "I must surrender my right of redemption. I cannot protect this inheritance for myself without causing harm to my own estate; I would risk the future of my own firstborn son. So, you must use my privilege, which I know I am unable to use myself."

(Ruth 4:7) 
Now, this was the established custom in Israel among close relatives when someone surrendered their right of redemption, in order that a transfer of property would be confirmed and legally binding: a man would take off his sandal and give it to his neighbor. This act served as a clear testimony of the surrender of rights in Israel.

(Ruth 4:8) 
So Boaz said to the close relative, "Then take off your sandal, as is the custom." And immediately, the kinsman took it off his foot.

(Ruth 4:9) 
Then Boaz solemnly declared to the elders and to all the people present, "You are all witnesses today that I have acquired everything that belonged to Elimelech, and everything that belonged to Chilion and Mahlon, all of it from the hand of Naomi."

(Ruth 4:10) 
"And I have also taken Ruth the Moabitess, who was the wife of Mahlon, to be my own wife. My purpose in doing this is to perpetuate the name of the deceased within his inheritance, so that his name will not be cut off from among his relatives, nor from the register at the gate of his hometown. I declare to you that you are indeed witnesses of this matter."

(Ruth 4:11) 
All the people who were sitting in the city gate, along with the elders, responded, "We are indeed witnesses! May the Lord make your wife, Ruth, like Rachel and Leah, who together built up the entire household of Israel. May you yourself become an example of strength and prosperity in Ephrathah, and may your name become celebrated and famous in Bethlehem."

(Ruth 4:12) 
"May your household become like the household of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah, because of the offspring the Lord will grant to you from this young woman, Ruth."

(Ruth 4:13) 
So Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife. He then went in to her, and the Lord granted her the grace to conceive and to bear a son.

(Ruth 4:14) 
Then the women of Bethlehem said to Naomi, "Praised be the Lord, who has not left you without a close relative today, so that his name may be celebrated and remembered in Israel! May this child be to you a full redemption and a comfort, and may his own name be cherished."

(Ruth 4:15) 
"May this child bring comfort to your soul and sustain you in your old age, for he has been born to your daughter-in-law, who deeply loves you. She is far better to you than if you had seven sons, for her loyalty and love surpass all."

(Ruth 4:16) 
Naomi then took the child herself, placing him in her lap, and she became his nurse and caretaker, tending to him as her own.

(Ruth 4:17) 
The neighbors and relatives, filled with joy and congratulating her, declared, "A son has been born to Naomi!" And they gave him the name Obed. This Obed later became the father of Jesse, who in turn was the father of David, the great king.

(Ruth 4:18) 
Now, these are the generations descending from Perez: Perez begat Hezron.

(Ruth 4:19) 
Hezron begat Ram, and Ram begat Amminadab.

(Ruth 4:20) 
Amminadab begat Nahshon, and Nahshon begat Salmon.

(Ruth 4:21) 
Salmon begat Boaz, and Boaz begat Obed.

(Ruth 4:22) 
Obed begat Jesse, and Jesse begat David.
